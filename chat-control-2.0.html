The question

Who should be able to read your messages?

Most people would say: me, the person I write to, and in some cases the police with a court order.

Where that line is drawn has been argued about for more than 30 years.

2

The dilemma

Two legitimate goals on one scale

The state has a duty to protect people, for example children, from serious crime.

People have a right to private communication (EU Charter, Art. 7 and 8).

More access for the state means less confidentiality, and the other way round. Every option gives something up.

Why privacy matters

“I’ve got nothing to hide”

The most common argument for surveillance, and three problems with it (Solove, 2007)

Small facts add up

Harmless data points, combined, show health, beliefs, relationships and routines.

It is about power

Whoever holds the data decides how it is used. The person concerned cannot check or correct it.

Rules change

What is harmless today can become a problem under a future government or in another country.

Solove, D. J. (2007). “I’ve Got Nothing to Hide” and Other Misunderstandings of Privacy. San Diego Law Review, 44, 745.

Why privacy matters

People change their behaviour when they feel watched

−20%

monthly views of 48 Wikipedia articles on terrorism-related topics after the Snowden revelations in June 2013.

Views kept falling in the following months. Researchers call this a chilling effect.

Penney, J. (2016). Chilling Effects: Online Surveillance and Wikipedia Use. Berkeley Technology Law Journal, 31, 117. Chart simplified.

Why privacy matters

Access built for the police can be used by others

Greece, 2004–05

The Athens Affair

Unknown attackers used the legal wiretap function in Vodafone Greece’s network to listen to about 100 phones, including the Prime Minister’s.

USA and worldwide, 2024–25

Salt Typhoon

Chinese state hackers got into US telecom networks, including systems used for court-ordered wiretaps. In 2025, 13 countries reported 200+ US organisations affected and 80+ countries targeted.

In Dec 2024 the FBI and CISA advised Americans to use encrypted messengers.

Security researchers conclude that a way in that works only for authorities cannot be built (Abelson et al., 2015).

Prevelakis & Spinellis (2007), IEEE Spectrum; joint advisory of 13 countries (27 Aug 2025); Abelson et al. (2015), Keys under doormats

Case study

Chat Control 1.0 and 2.0

2021

1.0 · Companies may scan

  • After EU extension of ePrivacy Directive to messaging apps

2.0 · Companies must scan

Five questions to weigh the dilemma

Principlist framework for cybersecurity ethics — Formosa, Wilson & Richards (2021), Lecture 2

24

Conclusion

What we conclude

1

The conflict is old. From the Clipper Chip to Chat Control, each new technology has raised the same question again.

2

Encryption moved it onto our devices. Hacking, scanning and backdoors all reach into the phone itself.

3

Targeted measures do better. In our assessment they meet four of the five principles better than scanning everyone, and they still catch criminals.

4

Infrastructure lasts. What is built for surveillance stays available to future governments and to attackers.
