Our verdict

BeneficenceBenefit humans, promote human well-being

Could find abuse material and offenders in encrypted chats, where reports have fallen

Benefit is unproven: more reports do not show more children protected, and offenders can switch channels

Open

Non-maleficenceNo intentional harm, lives not worse overall

Stopping the spread of abuse images could prevent further harm to victims

Not the aim, but foreseeable: are lives worse overall? Possible harm: weaker security for all, wrongly flagged users, fewer confidential spaces, later misuse

Open

AutonomyInformed decisions about how technology is used

Victims’ privacy: images of their abuse spread without their consent

Privacy invaded without a real choice: messages are scanned before end-to-end encryption can protect them

Not met

JusticeFairness, equality, impartiality, equal access

Solidarity with victims, and the same rules apply to all users

Everyone is treated as a suspect without cause, and carries the burden for a few offenders

Not met

ExplicabilityIntelligible, transparent, clear who is accountable

One central EU Centre receives all reports, so there is a defined point of accountability

Lists and AI cannot be checked and are secret; responsibility is split between providers, EU Centre and police
